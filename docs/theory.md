# Core Theory

A narrative links selected facts and interpretations into an account of what happened, why it matters, and what might happen next. The framework asks which parts of that account an audience notices, which expectations change, and whether those changes lead to measurable decisions.

| Stage | Research question | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information | What became available, when, and to whom? | Original documents, timestamps, and first-hand observations |
| Narrative | What interpretation connects the information to a possible outcome? | Recurring claims, stated mechanisms, and alternative interpretations |
| Attention | Which audience encountered or sought out the interpretation? | Qualified reach, search activity, and distribution across channels |
| Expectations | What did the audience expect before and after exposure? | Before-and-after surveys, forecasts, and explicit commitments |
| Capital | Which resources were committed, and when? | Approved budgets, verified allocations, and transaction records |
| Behavior | What did the audience actually do? | Adoption, retention, purchasing decisions, and workflow changes |

The sequence is a research model. Stages can overlap, run in reverse, or fail to materialize. An investment may attract attention, and repeated use may strengthen a narrative. A causal claim needs evidence that separates the proposed mechanism from competing explanations.

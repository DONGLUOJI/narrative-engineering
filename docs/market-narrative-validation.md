# Market Narrative Validation

This is an investment-research application of the [Narrative Engineering framework](theory.md). It follows a market claim from its first recorded source through attention, expectations, capital commitments, and observed behavior. Each step has its own evidence and can be reviewed on a different timeline.

## Research procedure

1. **Capture the claim.** Preserve the original source, publication date, first observation date, exact claim, and named or inferred entities. Record inference as interpretation. Use social posts to discover claims, then trace material assertions to primary documents where available.
2. **State the mechanism.** Start from the end product and work back to the component, process, or supplier that could constrain output. Specify what demand has to materialize, who can supply it, what alternatives exist, and when any impact should be observable.
3. **Track the stages separately.** Log discussion across independent sources for attention, forecasts or management guidance for expectations, and approved budgets or binding orders for capital. Track deliveries, utilization, repeat orders, or continued use as behavior. Reposts of a single claim count as repeated exposure to the same source. Distinguish a pilot or expression of interest from a binding commitment.
4. **Write the strongest countercase.** Check substitution, customer self-supply, qualified competitors, extra capacity, regulatory changes, and demand timing. Set explicit thresholds, a review date, and the evidence that would weaken or reverse the claim.
5. **Review against the original record.** Preserve the original forecast and append dated observations. Update the status to supported, weakened, contradicted, or unresolved. Evaluate the evidence for the mechanism separately from the direction of the market price.

## Narrative research card

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Original information | Source link, publication date, first observation date, direct observation, and source type |
| Market narrative | One testable claim, audience, baseline expectation, proposed mechanism, predicted outcome, and deadline |
| Evidence | Dated observations for attention, expectations, capital, and behavior, with source links and limitations |
| Counterevidence | Strongest alternative explanation, falsification conditions, and data gaps |
| Follow-up | Next review date, observable milestone, status, and revision history |

## Worked example

**Hypothetical case.** A robotics trade-show demo attracts discussion about precision ball screws. The research claim is that a planned move from pilot production to volume production will create sustained shortages of a specified grade over the next two reporting cycles.

- **Attention and expectations.** Track independent coverage and dated production guidance. Check whether reports repeat the same demo or add independently sourced information.
- **Capital and behavior.** Look for committed procurement or production budgets, followed by verified delivery volumes, utilization, and supplier lead times. Record mechanisms that could explain longer lead times, such as a temporary production interruption.
- **Counterevidence and review.** Before observation, define what qualifies as sustained tightness, which production milestone must occur, and the review date. A qualified substitute at scale, postponed production, or stable lead times despite rising output would weaken the claim. If equivalent supply or procurement data are unavailable by the review date, record the status as unresolved.
